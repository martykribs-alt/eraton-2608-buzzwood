Floor plans

Scale: 1 square = 5′ · plans drawn to the 20′ × 25′ footprint

Lower level · the home

500 SF · step-free
  • Everything for daily life on one level: bedroom, bath, kitchen, laundry
  • Roll-in shower and a 5′ turning circle in the bath; 36″ doors throughout
  • Side entry off a level landing, reached by an 18′ ramp

Upper level · guest suite

500 SF · own entry
  • For a caregiver, family, a guest, or the owner's own use
  • Kitchenette only: sink, under-counter fridge, microwave; no range or cooktop
  • Own entry from the exterior stair; front deck with treetop views

Section at the flood line

Heights in feet above existing grade · revised BFE basis (R-16)

The lowest floor sits 3′ above existing grade under the revised flood map. Balanced cut and fill raises the pad 1.5′, so the floor is 1.5′ above the ground around it and the entry needs only a short ramp. The lower map is not yet effective (R-13), and Philomath's freeboard rule is not yet loaded (R-09); both can move these heights.

Design principles

  • One 1,000 SF home on two levels: 500 SF below, 500 SF above
  • Everything for daily life on the step-free lower level
  • Upper suite for care, family or guests, with its own entry
  • Scandinavian-modern gable form; natural wood and durable cladding
  • Open, light-filled rooms that open to a patio and a deck
  • Homes set around a shared green

How it is priced

Each building sells as one home at about $450,000. That is the middle of an independent value check for a new 1,000 SF Philomath cottage ($395–475K) and matches the Cooper Lane appraisal method ($455/SF). No senior premium is assumed; the reservation list tests $435K, $475K and $515K.

Full numbers, constraints and decisions live in the Buzzwood feasibility record.
