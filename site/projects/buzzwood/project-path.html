What this map covers

Owner-side coordination among three parties: the owner, land use counsel and MKA. It shows who decides, who leads and who supports at each phase.

Architects, engineers, surveyors, specialist consultants and the contractor produce the work products. They appear where they lead. When MKA runs the diligence program, it scopes, procures, schedules and reconciles the specialists' reports; the specialists write them.

The Case
The owner's request to the approving authority: what is asked, who decides it, the evidence behind it, and its cost and schedule.
Relief path
One route around a binding constraint, such as a variance, a map revision, a code interpretation or a redesign. Several may be rated before the owner picks one.

Responsibility

DECIDES
Holds approval authority. Only the owner decides.
LEAD
Does the work and answers for the deliverable. One lead per deliverable.
SUPPORT
Supplies input the lead needs and answers for that input.
ON CALL
Not in the plan. Engaged when a named event occurs, then leads on that event.

Typical involvement

Critical path. This party's work usually sets the phase's pace.
Gating. Specific deliverables must land before the gate.
Scheduled. Planned, recurring work off the critical path.
Triggered. Engaged on an event.

These are typical patterns for an entitlement-driven project with a contractor under contract. Real dependencies vary, and any party can move onto the critical path when an issue lands in its lane.

Where counsel and MKA hand off

  1. Acquisition: counsel's land use report → MKA turns it into constraints, milestones, risks and a recommended path, each traced to the report.
  2. Planning: MKA builds the Case → counsel writes the narrative, files it and argues it.
  3. Conditions: each condition enters MKA's register when proposed and updates as it is accepted, contested or modified. The final decision locks the accepted set.
  4. Any phase: MKA flags a condition that won't price, draw or build → counsel files the modification.
  5. Construction start: the conditions, permit and baseline become the construction baseline; counsel moves to on-call.

Why bring MKA in early

Counsel is often the owner's first advisor, before a development team exists. With MKA alongside from the start, the approval strategy is built with cost, schedule and buildability evidence behind it, and the conditions that come out of the hearing already have someone carrying them to closure.

Counsel's readings of code are tracked as interpretations until staff confirms them, so design and budget decisions show which ones they rest on.
