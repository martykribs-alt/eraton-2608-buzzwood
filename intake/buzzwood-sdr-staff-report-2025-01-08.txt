The City received the Site Design Review application, file P24-08, on October 2, 2024; a pre-application meeting was held September 17, 2024, and the application was deemed complete December 10, 2024. [SR p.1, p.2]

No comments were received on the application. [SR p.1]

No conditions from other land use approvals apply to the parcel, and no variances were requested. [SR p.3]

The development property is tax lot 12612CD-01500 only; it is part of the Original Philomath Farm Lots plat. [SR p.22, p.23]

Total lot coverage with impervious surface is about 41.4 percent, under the 80 percent R-3 maximum; building height may not exceed 30 feet because of the Development Agreement. [SR p.3, p.4]

The units are treated as single-unit detached houses on one common lot, so the multifamily building orientation, building mass and street-facing elevation standards do not apply or are met by intent. [SR p.5, p.6]

Wetland indicators are found in the middle of the property, but it is unclear whether the whole area on Exhibit A is wetland; any required state or federal wetland permits must be approved before building permits. [SR p.10, p.11]

Common-area landscaping for each phase is enforced by withholding Certificates of Occupancy for that phase until its landscaping is complete. [SR p.12]

A detailed landscape plan with an irrigation plan or documented drought-resistant plants must be approved before any Certificate of Occupancy. [SR p.11, p.12]

No vehicle parking is required within half a mile of a bus stop, but installed parking must be hard-surfaced; the plan shows 24 shared spaces, 20 of them compact, and at least one must be ADA. [SR p.14, p.26]

New sidewalks to City standards must be built along the 15th Street frontage, and drop curbs at abandoned driveways reconstructed to full height. [SR p.15]

The existing curb cut will move south; the new one must be at least 15 feet from the 622 S 15th curb cut, and the applicant must obtain an access permit from Public Works. [SR p.8, p.9]

The 64-foot right-of-way on 15th Street is sufficient; no additional right-of-way dedication is required, but 8-foot public utility easements are required along the 15th Street frontage. [SR p.16, p.20]

The fire lane exceeds 150 feet and needs a turnaround meeting fire code, within a recorded fire lane easement to the City with owner maintenance. [SR p.9, p.27]

Sewer laterals and any capped existing laterals must be completed, tested and accepted before occupancy permits; common private sewer mains serving more than one structure must connect to the public sewer with a manhole. [SR p.16]

Phase 1 is planned on two standard water services and Phases 2 and 3 on a master meter, likely 3 inches or larger; meter size depends on fixture unit counts the developer must supply. [SR p.16, p.17]

Existing hydrants across 15th Street are too far away; fire flows of 1,000 gpm are required, and all water improvements for fire flow are the developer's sole responsibility. [SR p.17, p.18]

If the existing well remains, a reduced pressure backflow assembly is required on each water meter. [SR p.17]

Stormwater detention must meet PWDS 3.18 on private property with a recorded detention easement and maintenance agreement; Benton County MS4 water quality standards apply in addition, the more stringent governing. [SR p.18, p.19]

Franchise utilities must be underground and come from the fronting street unless a private easement is acquired. [SR p.20]

Per PMC 18.110.080.B, the building permit shall be obtained within 12 months of the land use approval, and construction must substantially conform to the approved preliminary plan as conditioned. [SR p.23]

The developer is responsible for the cost of all required public and private infrastructure, on-site and off-site. [SR p.24]

After the land use approval and any appeal period, the developer and engineer must hold a pre-design conference with City Public Works, the City Engineer and other agencies before submitting construction drawings. [SR p.24]

Building permits and Public Works construction permits will not be issued until engineered drawings are finally approved, a Developer-City construction agreement is executed, and a performance security is posted. [SR p.24]

Off-site and on-site easements must be approved by the City and recorded before final approval of the construction drawings. [SR p.25]

Occupancy permits will not be issued until all required improvements and conditions are complete and accepted by the City, with maintenance bonds and as-built drawings. [SR p.25]

The developer appears to propose three phases under this approval, and PWDS 1.8.a requires each phase's utility design to stand alone. [SR p.25]

Lot fills and fills in rights-of-way or fire lanes must be compacted and tested to City standards and the Oregon Structural Specialty Code. [SR p.25, p.26]

The proposal is to build finish floors at least 1 foot above the FEMA flood elevation, with construction drawings on the NAVD 1988 datum. [SR p.27]

A CBU mailbox must be installed at a location the postmaster accepts, with an ADA curb ramp within 50 feet. [SR p.28]

The approved construction sign, 5 feet tall and 9 square feet, set back 15 feet from the front lot line, may stand until construction is complete; the Site Design approval also approves the sign permit. [SR p.21; cover email]
